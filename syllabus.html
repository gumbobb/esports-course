Course syllabus

Everything about how the course runs, in one place. Read it in Week 0, then take the syllabus check in Module 0.

Course description

Introduction to Esports Management is a one-semester, fully online elective for high school students. It covers the structure of the esports industry, how teams and scholastic programs operate, event planning and production, marketing and sponsorship, ethics and player wellness, and career pathways. Students finish with a complete program or event proposal and a presentation. No gaming skill is required; curiosity about how the industry works is.

Course outcomes

  1. Explain how the esports industry is structured and how money and attention move through it.
  2. Design the structure, roles, and practice plan for a scholastic esports team or program.
  3. Plan a competitive event from format and rules through budget, staffing, and run of show.
  4. Produce a basic live broadcast segment and a marketing plan for an event or program.
  5. Evaluate ethical, wellness, and governance issues in esports and recommend responsible practices.
  6. Build a career plan and a sponsorship or program proposal suitable for a real audience.

Each weekly module lists which of these outcomes it supports, and every assessment is tied to that week's objectives.

Materials and technology

  • A computer or Chromebook with a current browser, and reliable internet. A phone works for reading and discussion but not for the capstone.
  • A free Google account (for the weekly checks, submission forms, and shared documents).
  • Padlet (free; no account required to post, but you will enter your name).
  • OBS Studio (free, Week 10 only). Install instructions are in Module 0.
  • No textbook. All readings are on this site or linked, and all linked materials are free.

Communication and feedback

  • Announcements post every Monday on the home page and by email. Read them first.
  • Questions board (Padlet) for anything that is not private. I check it every school day.
  • Email for private matters. I reply within two school days, usually sooner.
  • Office hours by video call, Tuesday and Thursday 3:30 to 4:30 PM, or by appointment.
  • Feedback on graded work is returned within 5 days of the due date. Every piece of feedback names at least one specific strength and one specific next step. Discussion feedback is posted as replies and a weekly summary.

Late work and make-up

  • Weekly assessments lose 10% per day late, up to three days. After three days, contact me; a zero is the last resort, not the first.
  • Discussions cannot be made up after Friday because the conversation has moved on, but one missed week per semester is dropped automatically.
  • If something in your life interferes (illness, family, work, internet outage), tell me before the deadline when you can. I will work with you. Silence is the only thing I cannot help with.
  • Weekly checks never close and are never late.

Artificial intelligence

AI tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Claude, and others) are part of this industry and part of this course. The rule is about learning, not about the tool.

Allowed, with disclosureNot allowed
Brainstorming ideas, outlining, asking for explanations of a concept, checking grammar and clarity on your own draft, generating practice questions for yourself.Submitting AI-written text, posts, or designs as your own work; using AI to answer a weekly check or write a discussion post; entering another person's private information into an AI tool.

Disclosure: when you used AI, add one line at the end of the assignment: "AI use: I used [tool] to [what it did]." Honest disclosure is never penalized. Undisclosed AI use is treated as academic dishonesty under school policy. Each module's "AI this week" box says exactly what is allowed for that week's work.

Academic integrity, respect, and privacy

  • Cite sources for anything you did not create, including images and clips. Module 0 shows how.
  • Esports communities have a toxicity problem. This course does not. Disagree with ideas, never with people. Harassment, slurs, or exclusion end participation in that discussion and are reported under school policy.
  • Do not post other students' names, handles, images, or work outside the course without their permission.

Accessibility

This course is designed to be usable by everyone: all videos are captioned, all images have descriptions, pages work with a keyboard and screen reader, and every assignment offers a choice of format (written, audio, video, or visual). If anything is not working for you, or you have an accommodation plan, tell me in the first week and I will adjust. You do not have to explain why.
