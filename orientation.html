Module 0 · Start here

Orientation: how this course works

Spend about an hour here before Week 1 opens. By the end you will know who I am, how we communicate, how the site works, what the tools are, and what it takes to do well. Then take the short orientation check so I know you are ready.

1. Who I am

I'm Mr. Hughes. I teach business and technology courses, coach scholastic esports, and spent ten years in the Army and several more in corporate operations before teaching. I have run tournaments, managed rosters, written sponsorship pitches, and sat through more bracket disputes than I care to count. I built this course because esports is a legitimate industry with legitimate careers, and most students never get a straight explanation of how it works.

What I value: effort over talent, questions over silence, and finished work over perfect work. What you can expect from me: a reply within two school days, feedback within five, and a course where you always know what to do next.

3. What the course covers

Fifteen weeks in five units. Each week has the same five parts: Learn (a reading and short videos), Check (ungraded practice questions), Do (a hands-on activity), Discuss (post and two replies), and Assess (one graded product with a rubric). The capstone proposal is built in pieces along the way.

Unit 1 · The Industry (Weeks 1 to 2)

History, ecosystem, careers, how money moves.

Unit 2 · Programs and Teams (Weeks 3 to 7)

Building a program, operations, coaching, wellness, scholastic leagues.

Unit 3 · Events and Production (Weeks 8 to 10)

Formats, logistics, budgets, broadcasting.

Unit 4 · Business and Ethics (Weeks 11 to 13)

Marketing, sponsorship, governance, inclusion, privacy.

Unit 5 · Careers and Capstone (Weeks 14 to 15)

Pathways, portfolios, final proposal and presentation.

4. How to get around the site

  1. The top menu is the same on every page: Home, Syllabus, Start Here, Modules, Capstone, Help.
  2. The course map on the left lists every week. The page you are on is highlighted in orange.
  3. Every module page has the same sections in the same order: objectives, Learn, Check, Do, Discuss, Assess, checklist. Jump to any section with the headings.
  4. Buttons open outside tools (Google Forms, Padlet, YouTube) in a new tab. Close the tab to come back.
  5. The checklist at the bottom of each module is the week's to-do list. If it is checked, you are done.
  6. Keyboard users: press Tab to move through links, and use the "Skip to main content" link at the top of each page.

5. Step-by-step guides to the tools

Weekly checks and submissions (Google Forms)

  1. Click the green "Open the check" button in any module. It opens in a new tab.
  2. Enter your first and last name in the first box. Every form asks for it.
  3. Answer the questions. Click Submit, then "View score" to see explanations.
  4. Retake as many times as you want. Only the submission forms (for graded work) are one-time.
  5. For a submission form: paste your Google Doc or Slides link, or upload the file, then Submit. You will get a confirmation email.

Discussions (Padlet)

  1. Click the purple "Open the discussion board" button. No login needed.
  2. Click the plus button (bottom right). Type a title that is your name.
  3. Write your post, or click the microphone or camera icon to record audio or video instead.
  4. Click Publish. To reply, click "Add comment" under a classmate's post.
  5. Your post is due Wednesday; two replies are due Friday.

Shared documents and peer review (Google Docs)

  1. Open the template link in the module. Choose File, then Make a copy.
  2. Rename it "Week # - Your Name." Work in your copy.
  3. To share for peer review: click Share, set "Anyone with the link" to Commenter, copy the link.
  4. To review a classmate: highlight text and click the plus icon to leave a comment. Use the sentence starters in the module.

Broadcast software (OBS Studio, Week 10 only)

  1. Download from obsproject.com (free, Windows, Mac, Linux). School Chromebook users: Week 10 has an alternative using a browser-based tool.
  2. Run the Auto-Configuration Wizard on first launch and choose "Optimize for recording."
  3. Week 10 walks through scenes, sources, and recording with screenshots. You will never need to go live.

6. How to succeed in this course

Log in every school day

Ten minutes a day beats three hours on Saturday. Announcements, replies, and deadline notes all live here.

Follow the weekly rhythm

Learn Monday and Tuesday, post Wednesday, build Thursday, reply Friday, submit Saturday. Nothing is ever due on Sunday.

Use the rubric before you submit

Every graded task shows exactly what "exceeds" looks like. Read it first, not after.

Speak up early

I cannot see your face. If you are confused, behind, or stuck, say so the day it happens. I have never once been annoyed by a question.

Post like a professional

Full sentences, a real position, and evidence from the week. Treat the discussion board like a team meeting, not a chat.

Make it yours

Almost every assignment lets you choose the game, the team, or the format. Pick what you care about. It shows.

8. Late work, make-up, and AI rules

  • Late assessments: minus 10% per day for up to three days, then talk to me. Life happens; tell me before the deadline when you can.
  • Discussions cannot be made up after Friday, but your lowest discussion week is dropped.
  • Weekly checks never close.
  • AI: allowed for brainstorming, outlining, explanations, and polishing your own draft, always with a one-line disclosure. Not allowed for writing your submissions, posts, or checks for you. Each module's "AI this week" box gives the specifics. Full policy is in the syllabus.

9. Community expectations

Esports has a reputation for toxic chat. This course is where you practice the opposite, because every job in this industry depends on it. Disagree with ideas, not people. No slurs, no harassment, no gatekeeping about which games are "real" esports. If the optional class Discord is used, the same rules apply and I am in it.

10. Module 0 tasks (5% of your grade, due end of Week 1)

  1. Read the syllabus and take the syllabus and orientation check (Google Forms, 10 questions, retake until 100%).
  2. Post your introduction on the Introductions board: your name, the game or esport you follow (or none yet), one thing you hope to learn, and a photo or image that represents you. Text, audio, or video. Reply to two classmates.
  3. Open the Week 1 check and submit a practice response so you know the form works.
  4. Optional: email me one thing you want me to know about how you learn best.
