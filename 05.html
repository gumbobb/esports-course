Coaching and Player Development

Feedback that improves play, VOD review, and a growth mindset culture.

Why this week mattersCoaching is a teaching job. The feedback skills here transfer to every team, classroom, and workplace you will ever be in.

This week you will be able to

1. Run a structured VOD review using a repeatable protocol (goal, observe, name, plan).
2. Give specific, actionable, respectful feedback to a player and distinguish it from criticism.
3. Describe how a coach builds a growth mindset culture and identify three practices that undermine it.

Supports course outcomes: Outcome 2

Learn

Read (about 15 minutes)

Coaching esports looks like coaching anything else once you strip away the game. The coach's job is to help players see what they cannot see while they are playing, and to turn that into a change they can make next time. The tool for this is VOD (video on demand) review: recording matches and watching them back with a purpose.

Unstructured review ('let's watch the loss') produces blame. Structured review produces learning. A simple protocol: set one goal before pressing play (we are watching our rotations, nothing else); observe without commentary; name what happened in neutral language; plan one specific change to practice. One goal per session. Players who are asked to fix six things fix none.

Feedback has a shape. Specific beats general ('you rotated late at 4:10 after the first pick' beats 'your rotations are bad'). Actionable beats evaluative (what to do next, not a grade). Respectful means the feedback is about the play, not the person, and is delivered in a way the player can hear. The same rules apply to feedback from teammates, which a coach has to teach explicitly because most players learned feedback from ranked chat.

Culture is built in the small moments. Coaches who praise effort and specific improvements, who treat losses as information, and who model taking feedback themselves get players who keep improving. Coaches who rank players publicly, punish mistakes, or let the best player set the tone get a team that stops taking risks. Carol Dweck's growth mindset research is the usual reference; in esports the evidence shows up every season.

Do: VOD Review Protocol in Action

Individual, applied 45 minutes

Purpose: Practice the protocol on real footage so it becomes a habit rather than a definition.

  1. Find a recorded match (your own, a friend's with permission, or a public professional VOD).
  2. Set one goal for the review and write it down.
  3. Watch 10 minutes and log three observations in neutral language with timestamps.
  4. Write one specific change you would ask the player or team to practice.
  5. Submit the log through the Week 5 form.
ChoiceUse any title and any footage. You do not need to play the game to review it; pick something observable like positioning or communication.

A good post is 5 to 8 sentences, takes a position, and uses something from this week's reading or video. A good reply asks a question or adds an example; "I agree" does not count. Posts can be text, a 60-second voice note, or a short video.
