Week 4 of 15 · Unit: Programs and Teams

Team Operations

Tryouts, rosters, roles, practice design, and the paperwork that keeps a team running.

Why this week mattersA team is a small organization. The habits you build here (fair selection, clear roles, planned practice) are the same habits that run a professional org.

This week you will be able to

1. Design a fair tryout and roster selection process with written criteria.
2. Describe the in-game and support roles for one title and match them to player strengths.
3. Build a two-week practice plan that balances scrims, review, and individual improvement.

Supports course outcomes: Outcome 2

Learn

Read (about 15 minutes)

Tryouts set the tone for everything that follows. Fair means written criteria shared in advance, evaluators who score the same things the same way, and a decision that can be explained to the student who did not make it. Rank is one input; coachability, communication, and attendance history matter at least as much, because the best-ranked player who skips practice costs you more than a weaker player who shows up.

Rosters need depth. A five-player title needs at least seven on the roster: injuries, eligibility, and life happen. Each title has defined in-game roles (duelist, support, in-game leader, and so on), and a roster is built around complementary roles, not five of the same. Off the server, a team needs a captain who runs communication, an analyst or VOD reviewer, and someone handling scheduling.

Practice should be planned, not just played. A good week mixes scrims against outside teams, review of recorded matches (VOD review) with specific goals, individual skill work, and short team meetings about communication and roles. Playing ranked for three hours is not practice; it is playing. The coaching week (Week 5) goes deeper on feedback; this week you build the calendar.

The paperwork is unglamorous and necessary: eligibility checks, parent permission, codes of conduct, attendance tracking, and a season calendar shared with families. Programs die from missed eligibility paperwork more often than from losing.

  2. Why should a five-player roster carry at least seven players?
  3. What is the difference between practice and playing?
  4. Name two off-server roles a team needs.
  5. Which piece of paperwork most often ends a team's season?

Do: Tryout Scorecard

Individual, template 40 minutes

Purpose: Write the evaluation criteria before the tryout, so selection is fair and explainable.

  1. Pick a title and list its in-game roles.
  2. Create a scorecard with at least five criteria (mechanical skill, game sense, communication, coachability, attendance) and a 1 to 4 scale for each.
  3. Write one sentence describing what a 4 looks like for each criterion.
  4. Add a short policy: how ties are broken and how a cut player is told.
  5. Submit through the Week 4 form.
ChoiceBuild it for any title, or for a general tryout that works across titles.

Discuss

Padlet discussion Post by Wednesday, two replies by Friday

Prompt: A top-ranked player has a history of toxic chat. Do they make the roster? Decide, explain, and describe what you would require if you said yes.

A good post is 5 to 8 sentences, takes a position, and uses something from this week's reading or video. A good reply asks a question or adds an example; "I agree" does not count. Posts can be text, a 60-second voice note, or a short video.

Assess: Two-Week Practice Plan

Individual plan 25 points Due Saturday 11:59 PM

A two-week practice calendar for one team: each session labeled by type (scrim, VOD review, skill work, team meeting), with a goal for each session and the total weekly hours. Include a one-paragraph rationale for the balance you chose.

Submit as: a calendar table, slide, or spreadsheet, or a captioned 3-minute video walkthrough. Share the file or link through the Week 4 submission form.
