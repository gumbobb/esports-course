Week 10 of 15 · Unit: Events and Production

Broadcasting and Production

OBS, scenes, overlays, casting, and getting a watchable stream out of a school lab.

Why this week mattersThe broadcast is how an event reaches everyone who is not in the room, and it is the single most employable skill in this course.

This week you will be able to

1. Set up OBS Studio with scenes and sources for a basic two-scene broadcast (game plus caster camera, intermission).
2. Explain the roles in a broadcast crew (producer, observer, play-by-play, color, graphics) and what each does during a match.
3. Record a short captioned broadcast segment with a clean overlay and audible commentary.

Supports course outcomes: Outcome 4

Learn

Read (about 15 minutes)

A broadcast is a production with a crew, not one person with a webcam. The producer runs the show and calls scene changes. The observer controls the in-game camera so viewers see the action. The play-by-play caster narrates what is happening; the color caster explains why it matters. Graphics handles overlays, scores, and lower thirds. In a school, these are student roles, and they are the roles most likely to lead to jobs.

OBS Studio is the free standard. It works in scenes (layouts) made of sources (game capture, camera, images, text, browser overlays). A basic broadcast needs a game scene with a caster camera in the corner and a score overlay, an intermission scene with a 'be right back' graphic and music, and maybe a starting-soon scene. Audio is where beginners fail: casters too quiet, game too loud, music over voices. Set levels before you go live and record a test.

Overlays are branding and information: team names, score, round, sponsor logos. Free overlay templates exist, and a simple clean overlay beats a busy one every time. Keep text readable at phone size, because most viewers are on phones.

Casting is a craft. Play-by-play keeps energy and tells viewers where to look. Color fills the gaps with context: why this strategy, what this player is known for, what happens if this fails. Good casters prepare notes on teams and players before the match. Great casters make a viewer who has never played the game care about what happens next. Record yourself, listen back, and cut the filler words.

Do: OBS Scene Build

Individual, hands-on 60 minutes

Purpose: Get hands on the actual tool. Reading about OBS is not knowing OBS.

  1. Install OBS Studio and run the auto-configuration wizard (choose recording). Chromebook users: use the browser-based alternative linked in the submission form.
  2. Build a game scene: a screen or window capture, a camera source (or an image placeholder), and a text source with two team names and a score.
  3. Build an intermission scene with an image and a text source.
  4. Record 60 seconds switching between the two scenes.
  5. Submit the recording (or screenshots of both scenes if recording fails) through the Week 10 form.
ChoiceCapture any game, a video, or even a slideshow; the skill is the scene build, not the content.

Discuss

Padlet discussion Post by Wednesday, two replies by Friday

Prompt: Watch 10 minutes of any esports broadcast. What did the production do well, and what would you change? Be specific: a scene, an overlay choice, an audio issue, a casting moment. Reply with a suggestion for a classmate's example.

A good post is 5 to 8 sentences, takes a position, and uses something from this week's reading or video. A good reply asks a question or adds an example; "I agree" does not count. Posts can be text, a 60-second voice note, or a short video.

Assess: Broadcast Segment

Individual production 25 points Due Saturday 11:59 PM

A 3 to 5 minute recorded broadcast segment: an intro on your starting-soon or intermission scene, at least two minutes of gameplay footage (yours, a classmate's with permission, or public footage with credit) with live or voiced-over commentary, a visible overlay with team names and score, and an outro. Captioned. Include a short written crew plan: who would do what if this were live.

Submit as: an MP4 or an unlisted YouTube link, plus the crew plan as text. Share the file or link through the Week 10 submission form.
