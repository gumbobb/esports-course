Week 11 of 15 · Unit: Business and Ethics

Marketing and Social Media

Brand, audience, content calendars, and community rules that keep a program's online presence safe and useful.

Why this week mattersNobody comes to an event they have not heard of, and no sponsor funds a program they cannot see. Marketing is how a program exists outside its own room.

This week you will be able to

1. Define a program or event's brand (name, visual identity, voice) and target audience.
2. Build a four-week content calendar across two platforms with a purpose for each post.
3. Write community guidelines for a program's social accounts that comply with school policy and student privacy.

Supports course outcomes: Outcome 4

Learn

Read (about 15 minutes)

Marketing starts with two questions: who are we trying to reach, and what do we want them to do? For a scholastic program the audiences are students (join), families (support), administrators (fund and approve), and community and sponsors (partner). Each wants something different, and a single generic post reaches none of them well.

Brand is consistency: a name, a logo, colors, and a voice that show up the same way everywhere. Students are good at this when given structure. The voice for a school program is enthusiastic and professional; it is not the voice of a pro org's trash-talk account, and the line matters because a principal will see it.

A content calendar turns 'post more' into a plan. Four weeks, two platforms, two to three posts a week, each with a purpose: recruit, inform, celebrate, thank a sponsor, show behind the scenes. Match highlights, player spotlights (with permission), event announcements, and sponsor shout-outs are the staples. Short video outperforms everything else on most platforms. Schedule posts; do not rely on remembering.

Guidelines protect everyone. Who has the account password, who approves posts, what never gets posted (student last names, schedules that reveal locations, anything about a student without consent), how comments are moderated, and what happens when someone is harassed. School district social media policy applies and usually comes first. A written guideline is also what you hand a new student media manager on day one.

Check your understanding

A short, ungraded five-question check in Google Forms. Take it as many times as you like; you will see the correct answer and a short explanation after each question.

Questions in this week's check
  1. Name the four audiences of a scholastic program and what each wants.
  2. What makes a brand consistent?
  3. Why should every post have a stated purpose?
  4. Name three things that should never be posted about students.
  5. Who should approve posts for a school program's account?
Open the Week 11 check (Google Forms)

Do: Brand Board

Individual, design 45 minutes

Purpose: Make the brand concrete so the content calendar has something consistent to apply.

  1. Choose your capstone program or event (or invent one).
  2. Create a one-page brand board: name, logo or wordmark, two or three colors with hex codes, one font, and three sentences describing the voice.
  3. Add two sample post mockups (one recruit, one celebrate).
  4. Note which school policy rules apply to the account.
  5. Submit through the Week 11 form.
ChoiceUse Canva, slides, a drawing, or paper and photo. Design skill is not graded; consistency and purpose are.

Discuss

Padlet discussion Post by Wednesday, two replies by Friday

Prompt: Find a scholastic or collegiate esports account you think does marketing well. What are they doing that works, for which audience? Reply to a classmate with one thing their example could borrow from yours.

A good post is 5 to 8 sentences, takes a position, and uses something from this week's reading or video. A good reply asks a question or adds an example; "I agree" does not count. Posts can be text, a 60-second voice note, or a short video.

Open the Week 11 discussion board (Padlet)

Assess: Four-Week Content Calendar and Community Guidelines

Individual plan 25 points Due Saturday 11:59 PM

A four-week content calendar for two platforms (8 to 12 posts total) with date, platform, post type, purpose, audience, and a one-line draft for each, plus a one-page community guidelines document covering account access, approval, what never gets posted, comment moderation, and harassment response. Reference your school or district's social media policy.

Submit as: a spreadsheet or table plus a document, or a slide deck. Share the file or link through the Week 11 submission form.
