Capstone Week: Finish, Present, Reflect

Final proposal, presentation, and a look back at what you can do now that you could not do in Week 1.

Why this week mattersA finished proposal in front of a real audience is the whole point. Everything else was practice.

This week you will be able to

1. Revise the capstone proposal using peer feedback and the final rubric.
2. Present the proposal to an audience in 5 to 7 minutes in an accessible format.
3. Reflect on growth across the six course outcomes with specific evidence.

Supports course outcomes: All six outcomes

Learn

Read (about 15 minutes)

Revision is where proposals become good. Start with the peer feedback from Week 14 and the final rubric on the Capstone page. Read your draft as the person you are pitching to: a principal with ten minutes, a sponsor with five, a venue manager with questions about power and parking. Cut what they do not need. Add what they will ask. Check every number.

The presentation is a performance of the proposal, not a reading of it. Open with the audience and the problem, show the plan, show the money, show that you have thought about what could go wrong, and end with the ask. Five to seven minutes is short; practice with a timer. Slides should carry images and key numbers, not paragraphs. If you record, caption it. If you present live, share the slides so a classmate can follow along.

Reflection is the last assessment and the one students most often skip. Do not. Look back at Week 1's ecosystem map and compare it to what you know now. For each of the six course outcomes, name one thing you made that proves you can do it. This is also the first draft of how you will describe this course to a college or an employer.

Finally: this course gave you a rules document, a run of show, a budget, a broadcast clip, a wellness policy, a code of conduct, a content calendar, a sponsorship proposal, and a complete program or event plan. That is a portfolio. Keep it.

Do: Revision Log

Individual, reflective 45 minutes

Purpose: Make revision visible and deliberate, so feedback actually changes the work.

  1. List every piece of peer feedback you received in Week 14.
  2. For each: accept, adapt, or decline, with one sentence of reasoning.
  3. List three changes you made from your own rubric read.
  4. Submit the log through the Week 15 form before your final.
ChoiceFormat the log however helps you: a table, a doc, or comments on your own draft.

Discuss

Padlet discussion Post by Wednesday, two replies by Friday

Prompt: Watch at least two classmates' presentations (links posted on the board by Wednesday). For each, post one thing that would convince a real decision maker and one question a decision maker would ask. This is the last discussion; make it a good one.

A good post is 5 to 8 sentences, takes a position, and uses something from this week's reading or video. A good reply asks a question or adds an example; "I agree" does not count. Posts can be text, a 60-second voice note, or a short video.
