Week 6 of 15 · Unit: Programs and Teams

Player Wellness

Bodies, sleep, screens, stress, and the responsibility a program has for the people in it.

Why this week mattersBurnout and injury end more esports careers than losing does. A program that ignores wellness is not a safe program, and administrators know it.

This week you will be able to

1. Identify the main physical and mental health risks for competitive gamers and the evidence behind each.
2. Design a wellness policy for a scholastic program covering practice limits, breaks, ergonomics, sleep, and mental health referral.
3. Evaluate a real team's wellness practices against your policy.

Supports course outcomes: Outcomes 2 and 5

Learn

Read (about 15 minutes)

Competitive gaming is physically demanding in ways that surprise people. Repetitive strain injuries in wrists and hands, posture problems from long sessions in poor chairs, eye strain, and disrupted sleep are common. Professional players have retired in their early twenties from wrist injuries. The fixes are not complicated: stretching, ergonomic setups, breaks every hour, and limits on daily hours. The hard part is making them a habit in a culture that celebrates grinding.

Sleep is the quiet one. Late-night practice and matches scheduled across time zones wreck sleep, and sleep loss measurably hurts reaction time and decision making, the exact skills players are trying to improve. A program that schedules practice until midnight is training players to be worse.

Mental health matters as much. Performance anxiety, tilt (the spiral of frustration that collapses decision making), toxic communication, and burnout are all common. Pro organizations now employ sports psychologists. Scholastic programs cannot, but they can teach tilt management, model healthy reactions to losing, and know who the school counselor is and when to refer. A coach is not a therapist, and knowing that line is part of the job.

A wellness policy makes all of this official: maximum practice hours per day and week, mandatory breaks, an ergonomics checklist, a sleep expectation around match days, a code of conduct that includes how teammates talk to each other, and a referral path for a student who is struggling. Written down and shared with families, it is also one of the strongest things you can show a principal.

Do: Ergonomics and Habits Audit

Individual, observational 30 minutes

Purpose: Apply the physical wellness guidance to a real setup so you see how far typical setups are from healthy ones.

  1. Audit a gaming or computer setup you have access to (your own, a lab, a friend's with permission).
  2. Check chair height, screen height and distance, wrist position, lighting, and break habits against the ergonomics guidance.
  3. Photograph or sketch the setup (no faces) and mark three things to change.
  4. Write the three changes and what each prevents.
  5. Submit through the Week 6 form.
ChoiceAudit a physical setup or, if you have none available, a professional player's publicly shown setup from a video.

Discuss

Padlet discussion Post by Wednesday, two replies by Friday

Prompt: Grind culture: many players believe more hours always equals more improvement. Using this week's material, make the case to a team that practice limits will make them better, not worse. What objection do you expect, and how do you answer it?

A good post is 5 to 8 sentences, takes a position, and uses something from this week's reading or video. A good reply asks a question or adds an example; "I agree" does not count. Posts can be text, a 60-second voice note, or a short video.

Assess: Program Wellness Policy

Individual policy document 25 points Due Saturday 11:59 PM

A one- to two-page wellness policy for a scholastic program: practice and screen-time limits, breaks, ergonomics checklist, sleep expectations around competition, communication and conduct expectations, and a referral path for mental health concerns. Written so a parent can understand it.

Submit as: a document, an infographic, or a captioned video explanation with a written summary. Share the file or link through the Week 6 submission form.
