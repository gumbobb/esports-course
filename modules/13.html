Week 13 of 15 · Unit: Business and Ethics

Ethics and Governance

Toxicity, cheating, inclusion, Title IX, data privacy, and who is responsible when something goes wrong.

Why this week mattersEvery program eventually faces a hard case. The programs that handle it well decided how before it happened.

This week you will be able to

1. Analyze a real ethics case in esports (toxicity, cheating, match fixing, harassment) and identify the stakeholders and failures.
2. Explain how Title IX, student privacy law, and school conduct policy apply to a scholastic program.
3. Write a code of conduct with a clear reporting and response process.

Supports course outcomes: Outcome 5

Learn

Read (about 15 minutes)

Esports inherited gaming culture's worst habit: anonymous cruelty. Toxic chat, harassment of women and minority players, and gatekeeping are common enough that many students who would love a program never join. A scholastic program is one of the few places that can change the norm, because it has adults, consequences, and a captive community. That starts with a code of conduct that is specific, enforced consistently, and modeled by coaches.

Cheating and integrity: aimbots and other software cheats, account sharing, smurfing (playing under a false lower rank), and in professional play, match fixing and betting. Scholastic programs mostly deal with the first three. Clear rules, monitored matches, and a culture where players police each other matter more than detection software.

Law and policy apply even when the activity is new. Title IX requires equal opportunity and a harassment-free environment regardless of sex, which has direct implications for how a program recruits and how it handles harassment. Student privacy law (FERPA in the U.S.) governs what can be shared about students, including on broadcasts and social media. Children's privacy law (COPPA) affects which platforms younger students can use. School and district conduct policies apply to online behavior in program spaces, including Discord.

Governance means someone is responsible. A program needs a reporting path (how a student reports a problem, to whom, with what confidentiality), a response process (who investigates, what consequences exist, how appeals work), and documentation. Write it down, share it with families, and follow it the same way for the star player and the newest member.

Do: Case Analysis

Individual, analytical 45 minutes

Purpose: Practice the hard thinking on a real case before you have to do it live.

  1. Choose a real esports ethics case (a harassment incident, a cheating scandal, a match-fixing case, a publisher or league governance failure).
  2. Summarize it in five sentences from at least two sources.
  3. Identify the stakeholders and what each failed to do or did well.
  4. Write what rule or process would have prevented or reduced the harm.
  5. Submit through the Week 13 form.
ChoiceAny case, any title, any level of play. Scholastic cases are welcome if reported publicly.

Discuss

Padlet discussion Post by Wednesday, two replies by Friday

Prompt: Zero tolerance or restorative response? For a first-time toxic chat incident by a 15-year-old, argue for the response you would choose, and explain how it would differ for a repeat incident or for harassment targeting a protected group.

A good post is 5 to 8 sentences, takes a position, and uses something from this week's reading or video. A good reply asks a question or adds an example; "I agree" does not count. Posts can be text, a 60-second voice note, or a short video.

Assess: Code of Conduct and Response Process

Individual policy document 25 points Due Saturday 11:59 PM

A code of conduct for a scholastic program (two pages maximum): expected behavior in matches, practice, Discord, and public spaces; prohibited behavior with examples; a reporting path with confidentiality; a response process with consequences and appeals; and a statement of how it aligns with Title IX, privacy law, and school policy. Written for students and families.

Submit as: a document, or an accessible infographic plus a document. Share the file or link through the Week 13 submission form.
