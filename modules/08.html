Week 8 of 15 · Unit: Events and Production

Event Planning I: Formats, Brackets, and Rules

Single elimination, double elimination, Swiss, round robin, seeding, and writing rules people can actually follow.

Why this week mattersFormat decisions determine how long an event runs, how fair it feels, and how many disputes you will handle. Get this right and the day runs itself.

This week you will be able to

1. Compare tournament formats by fairness, match count, time required, and spectator appeal.
2. Build a seeded bracket for a given number of teams using a bracket tool.
3. Write a rules document that covers match settings, timing, disputes, and conduct.

Supports course outcomes: Outcome 3

Learn

Read (about 15 minutes)

Every tournament format is a tradeoff between time, fairness, and drama. Single elimination is fast and dramatic, but one bad game sends a strong team home. Double elimination gives everyone a second chance and produces a fairer result at roughly double the matches. Round robin is the fairest and the slowest; everyone plays everyone. Swiss pairs teams with similar records each round, giving a fair ranking in few rounds, which is why it is common for large fields. Many events combine them: Swiss or groups to seed, then a bracket to finish.

Seeding matters. Random brackets can put the two best teams in the first round. Seeding by prior results or a quick qualifier spreads strength through the bracket so the best teams meet late. Bracket tools (Challonge, start.gg) handle the math, generate schedules, and let players see results live.

Rules are a document, not a vibe. A usable rules sheet covers: game version and settings, match format (best of 1, 3, 5), map or stage pool and selection method, check-in and forfeit timing, pauses and disconnects, what counts as cheating, how disputes are reported and who decides, and conduct expectations. Write for the stressed player at 9 PM in a close match, not for yourself.

Plan the math before you plan anything else. Teams times format equals matches; matches times average length (plus buffer) equals hours. Sixteen teams in double elimination with best-of-three matches is a very long day. Knowing that on paper is how you avoid finding it out at a venue.

Explore these sources

  • Challonge: free bracket builder Build a test bracket.
  • start.gg: tournament platform Browse a real event's bracket and rules.
  • Liquipedia: tournament formats Look up how a major event structured its stages.

Do: Build a Bracket

Individual, hands-on 40 minutes

Purpose: Use a real bracket tool so the format choices in the reading become something you can run.

  1. Create a free Challonge or start.gg account (or use the demo mode).
  2. Build a double-elimination bracket for 12 teams with seeding.
  3. Enter results for the first two rounds to see how the losers bracket fills.
  4. Screenshot the bracket and note the total number of matches.
  5. Submit the screenshot and match count through the Week 8 form.
ChoiceUse any bracket tool; a hand-drawn bracket with correct seeding is accepted if you have no access.

Discuss

Padlet discussion Post by Wednesday, two replies by Friday

Prompt: Which format would you choose for a one-day school tournament with 16 teams and six hours, and why? Show your match math. Reply to someone who chose differently and compare the tradeoffs.

A good post is 5 to 8 sentences, takes a position, and uses something from this week's reading or video. A good reply asks a question or adds an example; "I agree" does not count. Posts can be text, a 60-second voice note, or a short video.

Assess: Tournament Rules Document

Individual document 25 points Due Saturday 11:59 PM

A complete rules document for a tournament in one title: format and seeding method, game settings, match format and map or stage selection, check-in and forfeit timing, pause and disconnect policy, cheating definition, dispute process with who decides, and conduct expectations. Two pages maximum. Written for players, not organizers.

Submit as: a document; may be accompanied by a captioned video explaining the dispute process. Share the file or link through the Week 8 submission form.
