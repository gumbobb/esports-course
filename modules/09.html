Week 9 of 15 · Unit: Events and Production

Event Planning II: Logistics, Budget, Staffing, Run of Show

Venue or online platform, equipment, people, money, and the minute-by-minute plan for the day.

Why this week mattersThis is the week your capstone becomes operational. An event with a great format and no run of show is a long day of confused people.

This week you will be able to

1. Create a staffing plan with roles, counts, and shift times for an event.
2. Build an itemized event budget with revenue and expense lines and a contingency.
3. Write a run of show that sequences the event minute by minute with owners for each item.

Supports course outcomes: Outcomes 3 and 6

Learn

Read (about 15 minutes)

Events succeed on logistics. For an in-person event: a venue with enough power, network, seating, and sightlines; equipment with spares (one dead console should not stop a bracket); check-in; signage; food and water; and a plan for the parents and spectators who will show up. For an online event: a platform for brackets, a communication hub (usually Discord), match-reporting rules, and a way to handle disconnects that players will trust.

Staffing is roles times time. A small event needs an event lead, a bracket or admin desk, referees or match monitors (roughly one per two to four matches running), a tech lead, a broadcast crew if streaming, check-in staff, and someone whose only job is problems. Volunteers burn out; plan shifts and breaks. Students can fill almost every role with training, which is itself a learning outcome.

The budget has two sides. Expenses: venue, equipment rental or purchase, prizes, food, printing, insurance or permits where required, broadcast costs, and a contingency of 10 to 15 percent for the things you did not think of. Revenue: entry fees, sponsorship (Week 12), concessions, merchandise, donations, and in a school, activity funds. The budget should balance before you commit to a date.

The run of show is the document that makes the day calm. It lists every block from setup to teardown with start time, duration, what happens, who owns it, and what they need. Doors, check-in close, player meeting, round one start, breaks, finals, awards, teardown. When something slips, the run of show tells you what to compress. Build it with buffers; nothing in esports starts on time the first time.

  2. Roughly how many referees per running match?

  4. What does a run of show entry contain?
  5. What is the most common reason events start late?

Do: Run of Show Draft

Individual, planning 45 minutes

Purpose: Sequence a real day so you discover the gaps before the day does.

  1. Choose an event: your capstone event (Track B) or a one-day 8-team tournament (Track A or anyone).
  2. List every block from setup to teardown.
  3. For each block: start time, duration, what happens, owner, what they need.
  4. Add buffers and mark the two blocks most likely to slip.
  5. Submit the table through the Week 9 form.
ChoicePlan an in-person event, an online event, or a hybrid. The run of show looks different for each; pick the one closest to your capstone.

Discuss

Padlet discussion Post by Wednesday, two replies by Friday

Prompt: Online or in person? For a scholastic tournament, argue which produces the better experience for players and which is more realistic to run, and whether those are the same answer. Use one logistics detail from the reading as evidence.

A good post is 5 to 8 sentences, takes a position, and uses something from this week's reading or video. A good reply asks a question or adds an example; "I agree" does not count. Posts can be text, a 60-second voice note, or a short video.

Assess: Capstone Checkpoint 2: Operations Draft

Capstone piece 30 points Due Saturday 11:59 PM

Track A: program structure (from Week 3), season calendar, practice schedule, facilities and equipment plan, and a first-year budget. Track B: event format and rules summary, venue or platform plan, staffing plan with shifts, itemized budget with contingency, and run of show. Both tracks: a short list of risks and how you would handle each.

Submit as: a document or slide deck; tables welcome. Share the file or link through the Week 9 submission form.
