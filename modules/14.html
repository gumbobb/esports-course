Week 14 of 15 · Unit: Careers and Capstone

Careers and Pathways

The jobs that actually exist, how people get them, and building a portfolio that proves you can do the work.

Why this week mattersMost students who love esports will never be paid to play. Almost all of them could be paid to do something in this course.

This week you will be able to

1. Map at least ten esports and adjacent careers by function (operations, production, marketing, coaching, analytics, business) with typical entry paths.
2. Evaluate college esports programs, scholarships, and non-degree pathways against personal goals.
3. Build a portfolio outline and a resume entry that document skills from this course.

Supports course outcomes: Outcome 6

Learn

Read (about 15 minutes)

The esports job market looks like the entertainment and sports industries it grew out of: a small number of players and casters at the top, and a much larger number of people in operations, production, marketing, sales, community, coaching, analytics, design, and business roles. Those roles are where careers are. Many of them do not require being good at any game; they require being good at the work you have been doing for thirteen weeks.

Entry paths are practical. Volunteer at events, run the broadcast for a school program, manage a club's social media, intern with a collegiate program or local organizer, build a portfolio of things you have actually produced. Hiring managers in this industry care about what you have done far more than what you studied. A rules document, a run of show, a broadcast clip, and a sponsorship deck are a portfolio.

College matters, but not the way students expect. Over two hundred colleges have varsity esports programs, many with scholarships, and a growing number offer degrees or certificates in esports management, sports management, digital media, or business with an esports concentration. Playing scholarships are real and small; the better opportunity for most students is a program that gives them production, management, and event experience while they earn a degree in something employable.

Document everything. The portfolio you start this week is the thing you will show a college program, an internship coordinator, or an employer. Resume language translates course work into skills: 'planned and budgeted a 16-team tournament,' 'produced captioned broadcast segments in OBS,' 'wrote sponsorship proposals with tiered packages.' Those sentences are true after this course, and they are rare on a high school resume.

Questions in this week's check
  1. Where are most esports careers found?
  2. Name three entry paths that do not require being a top player.
  3. What should a student look for in a college esports program besides a playing scholarship?
  4. Turn 'ran the stream for our team' into resume language.
  5. What four artifacts from this course belong in a portfolio?

Do: Job Posting Analysis

Individual, research 40 minutes

Purpose: Read what employers actually ask for so your portfolio and resume answer it.

  1. Find five real esports or adjacent job or internship postings (Hitmarker, LinkedIn, a team's careers page).
  2. For each: title, function, required skills, preferred experience, and which of this course's weeks map to it.
  3. Identify the two skills that appeared most often.
  4. Write one sentence on which posting you would be closest to qualifying for in two years and what you would need to add.
  5. Submit through the Week 14 form.
ChoiceAny role, any level, any region. Internships count.

Assess: Career Plan and Portfolio Outline

Individual plan 25 points Due Saturday 11:59 PM

A career map of ten roles by function with entry paths; a comparison of two college or non-degree pathways against your own goals; a portfolio outline listing at least six artifacts (from this course or elsewhere) with a one-line description of what each proves; and three resume bullet points written in professional language.

Submit as: a document, slide deck, or a simple website or Google Site. Share the file or link through the Week 14 submission form.
